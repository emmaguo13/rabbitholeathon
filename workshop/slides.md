# what does housing and land ownership look like in china?

May 1990: Interim Regulation No.55

<!-- pause -->

```text
             /\
            /  \
           /____\
           | [] |     home: privately owned
           | __ |
           ||  ||
       ____||__||____
      |_____________| land: state-owned (urban)

      your residential land-use right: up to 70 years
```

<!-- pause -->

when you sell the home, the remaining land-use term transfers with it

<!-- pause -->

residential land-use rights renew automatically

<!-- speaker_note: "This diagram describes urban housing. Owning the home and holding a right to use the underlying land are distinct. The initial residential land-use grant can be up to 70 years. # what are the tangible affects of these ownership structures?

- how family wealth is maintained
- how cities are financed

<!-- pause -->

in the US:
- people take care of their property to maintain value
- it is hard for the local government to seize your land
- public schools, police, and roads are funded by annual property taxes

<!-- pause -->

in China:
- people see buildings as temporary
- state owns the land
- public infra is funded by selling 70-year land leases to developers

<!-- pause -->

cities were heavily incentivized to build massive residental complexes to generate revenue -> property market is cooling, local govs face debt crises

<!-- end_slide -->

# people r fading real estate ownership in china

- property values have plunged, wiping out trillions in paper wealth
- stimulus packages
- real estate is no longer a good way to store value
- gov is implementing property taxes

<!-- end_slide -->

# why did china do this?

- socialism
- easy for gov to plan cities, build highways, construct public transit
- massive gov revenue
- control over the private sector

<!-- end_slide -->

# timeline

```text
1950       land reform: peasants own their plots
  │
1950s      Mao: rural land becomes collective
  │
1978+      Deng era: households farm collective land - can benefit from profits
  │
1988–90    urban reforms: transferable land-use rights
```

private farming incentives can coexist with public land ownership
